# Naming Research

Status: Working shortlist, not cleared
Last updated: 2026-11-09
Related: [`product-spec.md`](product-spec.md)

## 1. Brief

The name should convey the core value: **fast, thoughtless capture** of whatever is in your head, into a place that **gives it back later** in a more useful form than a flat thread. Names that convey only "throw it in" describe a junk drawer, where things get lost. The strongest names suggest both putting something away and getting it back.

## 2. Criteria

1. **Works as a verb.** "Just ___ it" is the habit being built.
2. **Short and easy to say aloud.**
3. **Suggests getting things back**, not only storing them.
4. **Available in the productivity category.** The App Store subtitle can carry meaning (e.g., "[Name]: Note to Self"), so the bare word needn't be descriptive. It does need to be trademark-clear.
5. **Passes the target-user test.** Someone in the target audience would say it to a friend without embarrassment.

## 3. Key finding

**Plain words about saving things ("stash", "tuck", "save", "later", "note to self") are saturated** among iOS capture and save-for-later apps. Many of these apps appeared in 2025–26, as the category fills with indie and AI-assisted apps. Distinctive or coined names are much easier to trademark and to find in search. This also confirms risk R7 (crowded market) in the product spec.

## 6. Shortlist

1. **Jotsam** — clearest available, coined (strongest trademark category). Risk: people may not get it or spell it right ("Jotsum"). Subtitle helps: "Jotsam: Note to Self".
2. **Ferret** — best meaning of any candidate. Risk: KPTools Class 9 registration; mixed associations with the animal.
3. **Cubby** — fallback; crowded but no direct notes conflict found.

## 7. Limitations

- All findings come from web search plus third-party trademark sites, not USPTO TSDR. They're good enough to rule names out, not to clear them.
- Domain availability was **not** checked: registry lookups were blocked in the research environment.
- The App Store wasn't searched directly; results come from web-indexed listings and may miss apps.

## 8. Next steps

1. Target-user gut check: say the shortlist names to the original user and 3–5 peers.
2. Search USPTO directly ([tmsearch.uspto.gov](https://tmsearch.uspto.gov)), Classes 9 and 42, including spelling variants.
3. Search the App Store on a device for exact and similar names.
4. Check domains (`.app`, `.com`, and reasonable variants such as `getjotsam.com`).
5. Before launch: a trademark attorney's clearance search on the chosen name.
